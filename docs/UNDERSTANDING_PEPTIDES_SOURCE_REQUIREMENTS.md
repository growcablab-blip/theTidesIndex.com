# Understanding Peptides — what each unwritten chapter needs

Five of twelve chapters are written. Seven are not, and none of them is
blocked on writing time: each is blocked on a source this index does not hold.

This document says exactly what each one needs, so that a purchase can be
made against a specific requirement rather than against a general sense that
more sources would help.

**The short version.** Two books unlock six of the seven chapters:

1. A standard biochemistry text — unlocks chapters 1, 2, 3 and most of 4.
2. A standard pharmacology text — unlocks chapters 5 and 7, and completes 4.

Chapter 6 needs neither; it needs review literature, and it is the one chapter
that could arguably be written from what the register already holds.

---

## Why these chapters are not simply written

A patient-facing chapter that says "a peptide is a short chain of amino acids"
is not controversial, and this index still will not write it without a source.
The reason is not pedantry. This volume is read by people with the least
ability to check it, a sourced paragraph and an unsourced one set identically
on the page, and once one chapter is written from general knowledge there is no
line left to hold — the next chapter is about what peptides do in the body,
and that is where a plausible sentence becomes a medical claim.

So the rule is the same as everywhere else in the index: a statement resolves
to a location in a named source, or it is not made.

## Chapter one — What is a peptide?

**Scientific questions needing support**

- What distinguishes a peptide from a protein, and why the boundary is a
  convention rather than a fact of nature.
- What a peptide bond is, structurally.
- Why the distinction matters for how something is made, stored and measured.

**Source type needed.** A general biochemistry textbook, citable at page level.

**Candidates.** Lehninger *Principles of Biochemistry* (8th ed.) or Stryer
*Biochemistry* (9th ed.).

**Owner acquisition required.** Yes.

**Priority.** 1 — it is the first chapter, and the boundary question recurs in
the compound records.

---

## Chapter two — Amino acids, peptides, proteins

**Scientific questions needing support**

- The twenty proteinogenic amino acids as an alphabet.
- How sequence gives rise to structure.
- What changes as a chain gets longer.

**Source type needed.** The same biochemistry textbook.

**Candidates.** As chapter one — no separate purchase.

**Owner acquisition required.** No, if chapter one's book is bought.

**Priority.** 1 — same source, no extra cost.

---

## Chapter three — Peptides in the human body

**Scientific questions needing support**

- Endogenous peptide families and the roles they play.
- Why "the body makes it" is not an argument about safety or efficacy.

**Source type needed.** Biochemistry or physiology, covering endocrine and
signalling peptides.

**Candidates.** Lehninger covers this adequately for a patient-facing chapter.
A physiology text (Guyton & Hall, *Textbook of Medical Physiology*, 14th ed.)
would be better but is not required.

**Owner acquisition required.** No, if chapter one's book is bought.

**Priority.** 2 — this is the chapter most likely to overstate, so it needs
the strictest sourcing of the seven.

---

## Chapter four — How peptide signalling works

**Scientific questions needing support**

- Signalling as a message-and-receiver system.
- Why specificity matters.
- What a signalling molecule does not do.

**Source type needed.** Biochemistry for the mechanism, pharmacology for what
follows from binding.

**Candidates.** Covered jointly by the two books above.

**Owner acquisition required.** No, if both books are bought.

**Priority.** 2.

---

## Chapter five — Receptors and cellular communication

**Scientific questions needing support**

- Receptor binding as recognition.
- Selectivity, and what follows from a molecule binding more than one target.

**Source type needed.** A pharmacology textbook. This is the chapter the
biochemistry book cannot carry.

**Candidates.** Rang & Dale's *Pharmacology* (10th ed.) or Goodman & Gilman's
*The Pharmacological Basis of Therapeutics* (14th ed.).

**Owner acquisition required.** Yes.

**Priority.** 1 — the compound records use this vocabulary constantly
("agonist", "selectivity", "modulation") and nothing in the index defines it.
The same purchase also unlocks two chapters of *Peptide Science &
Applications*.

---

## Chapter six — Why peptides are studied

**Scientific questions needing support**

- What makes peptides attractive as a class to study.
- The practical difficulties that come with them.
- The difference between an area of active research and an established
  treatment.

**Source type needed.** Review literature on peptide therapeutics. Partly
available already: SRC-007 (*Advances in the Discovery and Development of
Peptide Therapeutics*) and SRC-010 (*Peptide-based Drug Discovery*) are held
and cover the difficulties and the development picture.

**Candidates.** No purchase needed. This chapter requires extraction work
against sources already on the shelf.

**Owner acquisition required.** No.

**Priority.** 3 — cheapest chapter to finish, and it needs editorial time
rather than money.

---

## Chapter seven — Routes of administration

**Scientific questions needing support**

- What each route demands of a molecule.
- Why oral administration is hard for peptides — the question several compound
  records raise and none answers.
- What a route implies for stability and handling.

**Source type needed.** Pharmacology (ADME) plus pharmaceutics. The
pharmacology text above covers absorption and bioavailability; SRC-010, held,
covers peptide-specific delivery problems.

**Candidates.** Pharmacology text above; optionally Banga (SRC-014, currently
the wrong work — see the acquisition queue) for formulation and delivery.

**Owner acquisition required.** Partly — the pharmacology text.

**Priority.** 2 — it closes a gap the compound records point at repeatedly.

---

## Recommended minimum purchase

| Buy | Unlocks |
|---|---|
| One biochemistry textbook (Lehninger 8th ed. preferred) | Chapters 1, 2, 3, and half of 4 |
| One pharmacology textbook (Rang & Dale 10th ed. preferred) | Chapters 5, 7, the rest of 4 — and two chapters of *Peptide Science & Applications* |

That is two books for six chapters. Chapter 6 needs no purchase.

**Please do not buy** a shelf of overlapping peptide-chemistry books. The
index already holds five, and they cover synthesis, purification and
characterisation well; what is missing is the general science underneath, and
two standard references supply it.

## After the books arrive

Each chapter still needs the normal pipeline: extract claims to exact page
locations, write the chapter from those claims, and put it through scientific
and clinical review. The books remove the blocker; they do not write the
volume, and nothing here should be read as an estimate that the chapters
appear the week the parcels do.
