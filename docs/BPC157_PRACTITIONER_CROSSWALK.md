# BPC-157 — practitioner claim crosswalk

What the practitioner sources say, what they cite for it, and what the cited
work actually is.

Screen: `bpc-157-pubmed-2026-09` — PubMed, 13 September 2026, 230 records.
Sources crosswalked: SRC-002 (Peptide Handbook), SRC-003 (Optimize Your Health
with Therapeutic Peptides), SRC-005 (The Complete Guide to Peptides).

---

## 1 · Why this exists

The handbook is a careful source. It does not claim human evidence, it says
throughout that its findings come from laboratory studies, and it supplies
twenty-two numbered references for its BPC-157 entry — more than most
practitioner references supply for anything.

The question a clinic actually has is different: **if I follow those citations,
where do I end up?**

The answer is that you end up in rats. Every one of the twenty-two is an animal
study or a review. Not one is a study in people, and the single citation given
for the most consequential claim in the entry — that the compound inhibits
tumour cell growth — is a conference abstract from 2004.

That is not a criticism of the source. It is the thing this index exists to make
visible, and it cannot be seen from the entry itself.

## 2 · Method

References 46–67 of SRC-002 were transcribed from the held copy and matched by
title against the screening ledger. Matching is by title rather than by
identifier because the handbook supplies no PubMed identifiers, and the
bibliography is reproduced from an imperfect scan.

Two references (48 and 57, 58 and 61) are near-duplicates of each other. They
are preserved as printed rather than merged, because how many distinct works a
source cites is part of what the crosswalk measures.

## 4 · Claim by claim

For each meaningful claim: does the source cite a primary paper, is that paper
in the screen, what model, and does the paper support the interpretation, the
route, the protocol, or only a mechanism?

### Tissue and wound healing — tendon, ligament, bone, muscle, skin

Cites 49–57. Animal and review. The primary paper closest to the claim (50,
PMID 21030672) is a rat tendon study.

**Supports:** that the effect has been observed in animal models.
**Does not support:** any human use, any route in humans, any regimen.
**Reclassified as:** preclinical mechanism and effect. Already labelled that way
on the record; the crosswalk confirms it rather than changing it.

### Angiomodulation — "increased when needed, decreased when needed"

Cites 51 and 58. PMID 27847966 shows increased vessel density in a chick
chorioallantoic membrane assay, restored blood flow in a rat hind limb, and
VEGFR2 upregulation in human endothelial cells in a dish.

**Supports:** pro-angiogenic activity in animal models and in human cells.
**Does not support:** the bidirectional claim. "Decreased when needed" is the
part that matters clinically and the cited work does not demonstrate it.
**Reclassified as:** a mechanism claim with the bidirectionality flagged as the
source's characterisation rather than the study's finding.

### Inhibits the growth of several tumour cell lines

Cites 66 only. A 2004 conference abstract, in one human melanoma cell line, not
indexed in PubMed and not obtainable here.

**Supports:** that somebody reported growth inhibition in one cell line, once,
in an abstract.
**Does not support:** anything about tumours in an animal, let alone a person.
**Reclassified as:** one side of a recorded, unresolved disagreement, with the
weight of its evidence stated — see §5.

### Counteracts tumour cachexia

Cites 47. PMID 29898649 is a review that *proposes* the application, and its
abstract says in terms "Before clinical trial, we introduced the evidence".

**Supports:** that the application has been proposed.
**Does not support:** that it works, in anything.
**Reclassified as:** a proposal, attributed, not an effect.

### Gastroprotection against ethanol, NSAIDs, corticosteroids, neuroleptics

Cites 53, 62, 64, 65. Four animal studies, and the best-supported group of
claims in the entry — this is the compound's original research programme and the
animal evidence for it is substantial and consistent.

**Supports:** a well-replicated animal finding.
**Does not support:** the leap to human gut conditions, which is the use most
often made of it.

### Neuroprotection, cardioprotection

Cites 59–61. Reviews and animal work. The QTc claim (cardioprotection) traces to
a review rather than to an electrophysiology study.

**Reclassified as:** preclinical, with the cardioprotection claim marked as
resting on a secondary source.

### Oral administration

No reference in the entry supports the oral route. The handbook gives an oral
regimen; the screen contains no study of oral bioavailability in any species,
and none in humans.

**Reclassified as:** a reported route with no supporting evidence of absorption,
already recorded as such and confirmed by the screen.

## 5 · Cancer: both sides, and what is behind each

The record keeps this open. What the screen adds is the weight on each side.

| | Position | Evidence behind it |
|---|---|---|
| **Caution** | The compound is pro-angiogenic; tumours need blood vessels | PMID 27847966 — chick membrane assay, rat hind limb, human endothelial cells. Real, replicated, preclinical. A 2025 review (PMID 40005999) raises tumorigenesis from this mechanism, as a speculation. |
| **Anti-tumour** | It inhibits tumour cell growth and counteracts cachexia | One 2004 conference abstract in a melanoma cell line, and a 2018 review proposing an application before trial. The principal research group's 2025 comment (PMID 41155565) asserts "prominent anti-tumor potential, in vivo and in vitro"; the in vivo work it refers to has not been traced here. |

The two sides are not symmetrical in evidence — and neither is resolvable from
what exists, because a cell line is not a person in either direction. Both are
recorded. Neither is a platform conclusion.

## 6 · What the crosswalk changed on the record

- **BPC-009**, a new claim stating what the handbook's citations lead to.
- **BPC-D-002** rewritten: the cancer question is now a four-position record
  with two research groups' published positions alongside the handbook's two,
  and it stays `unresolved`.
- Two new evidence gaps: the material used in the human studies, and the full
  texts of those studies.
- The oral route gap, already recorded, is now supported by a systematic
  absence rather than by the absence of a source in this library.

## 7 · What this crosswalk does not do

It does not read the twenty-two cited papers. It establishes what kind of study
each is, from the screening ledger, which is enough to answer "does following
these citations reach human evidence" and not enough to answer "does the cited
work support the specific claim made from it".

That second question needs the papers, and the priority order for reading them
is in `docs/PEPTIDE_RECONCILIATION_REPORT.md` §9.
