# SCIENTIFIC REVIEWER GUIDE

Five minutes. Read this once before your first packet.

---

## What The Tides Index is

An independent reference on peptide science, evidence and quality. Every
statement it publishes resolves to an exact page in a named source, and every
page states both what a test establishes **and what it does not**.

Nothing has been published. Twenty-seven statements are prepared and waiting for
a first scientific reviewer, which is what you are being asked to be.

---

## What you are being asked to assess

For each statement, whether:

1. **The source says what we say it says.** Every statement carries the passage
   it rests on, with a page number you can open.
2. **Our reading is fair** — and not stronger than the passage supports.
3. **The stated uncertainty covers what a reader ought to be warned about.**
4. **The scope is right** — population, material type, document type,
   jurisdiction, era.
5. **Nothing is asserted that the cited passage does not carry.**

## What you are **not** being asked to assess

- **Not to write or rewrite the content.** If something is wrong, request a
  change and say what is wrong. An editor revises it and it comes back to you.
- **Not to judge any product, supplier or certificate.** This index describes
  what tests establish; it does not rate anything.
- **Not to vouch for sources we do not hold.** If a point needs a source we lack,
  that is a gap to record, not a statement to approve.
- **Not to fill a gap from your own knowledge.** If you know something our
  sources do not establish, that is a source for us to acquire. Approving it
  would make your expertise into a citation, which is not what a citation is.

---

## The four things on every statement

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | The proposition as the index states it |
| **Evidence** | The passage it rests on, with source, page, and the kind of source |
| **Interpretation** | How the index reads that passage — *our* words, not the source's |
| **Uncertainty** | What the index records as unsettled |

**Scope** appears where a statement only applies to some cases — a requirement
that governs certificates for active pharmaceutical ingredients and not for
laboratory reports, for example. If a statement needs a scope and has none, that
is worth a change request.

**Evidence gaps** are listed separately. A gap is a statement the index
deliberately does **not** make because nothing it holds settles the point. Please
check these too: a gap wrongly worded as a finding is the same error as an
unsupported claim, pointing the other way.

---

## Your three actions

| Action | Use it when |
|---|---|
| **Approve** | The statement is fairly drawn from the cited passage, correctly scoped, and honestly qualified |
| **Request changes** | Something is wrong, overstated, mis-scoped, or missing a caveat. Say what; you are not expected to fix it |
| **Comment** | Worth recording without blocking |

A change request is not a rejection. It is the ordinary path.

---

## Approvals are bound to a version

**Your approval applies to the exact version you read.** Any later edit bumps the
version and your approval stops applying — automatically, in the database, not by
anyone's decision.

You will be asked again, and shown **what changed** since the version you
approved. This is deliberate: an approval that survived rewriting would not be an
approval of anything.

You will also see decisions already recorded, including automated ones. Automated
checks confirm that a citation resolves to the passage it claims. They are marked
as automated and **cannot** stand in for scientific review — the database refuses
to record an automated scientific approval at all.

---

## Publication

Approving does not publish. Publication additionally requires an approved source
check, and — for high-impact statements — stated uncertainty and a compliance
review. Those gates are database rules, not policy anyone can waive.

A statement can therefore be scientifically approved and still not appear. That is
normal.

---

## Disagreement

Where sources genuinely conflict, we record the disagreement and both positions
rather than picking a winner. If you think two sources conflict and we have
presented one as settled, say so — that is a change request, and the result is a
recorded disagreement rather than a deletion.

If you disagree with our reading and we disagree with you, your position is
recorded against the record. We do not resolve that by overruling you quietly.

---

## Corrections

Everything published carries a version and a correction history. If something
approved later turns out to be wrong, it is corrected publicly with the reason
recorded, and the correction names what changed. Nothing is edited away silently.

---

## Conflicts of interest

We record, for each reviewer, whether a relevant conflict has been disclosed.
Three states: disclosed with a note, none declared, or **not yet asked**. The last
is visibly different from the second, because an approval by someone nobody asked
should not read like one by someone who answered.

If you have a relevant interest — a consultancy, a holding, a role with a
supplier or manufacturer in this area — tell us and we record it. It does not
disqualify you from reviewing.

---

## If anything is unclear

The packet should answer its own questions. If you find yourself needing to know
how the database works to review a statement, that is our defect — please say so.
