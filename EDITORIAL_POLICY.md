# EDITORIAL POLICY

## Mission

The Tides Index explains peptide science clearly without turning uncertainty into certainty or practitioner opinion into established medicine.

## Editorial hierarchy

When sources disagree, do not “solve” the disagreement by averaging them. Display:
- who says what
- what evidence type supports it
- whether the underlying primary source was verified
- whether the disagreement may be explained by route, formulation, population, dose, study design, terminology, or date

## Required distinctions

### Approved use
Explicitly identify jurisdiction and source/date.

### Investigational human evidence
Describe the actual study population, design, route, schedule, and status.

### Preclinical evidence
Always say animal/in-vitro where applicable. Do not translate the regimen into human instructions.

### Practitioner reference
Attribute it by name/source. Do not write it as universal clinical consensus.

### Expert commentary
Identify the speaker/source/date and whether the statement was independently verified.

### Experiential/community
Do not use as support for efficacy/safety claims. It may be indexed as an observation or research question.

## Patient content

Patient-facing pages:
- explain rather than prescribe
- use plain language
- state uncertainty
- explain evidence strength
- encourage discussion with a qualified clinician
- do not expose dose/frequency protocol tables by default
- do not imply unapproved compounds are established therapies

## Practitioner content

Practitioner pages may display source-reported protocols when:
- source and location are known
- context/population/model is known
- evidence/source type is labeled
- uncertainty is preserved
- approved-label instructions are visually distinct from study or practitioner regimens

## Strong claims

Words such as:
- proven
- safe
- effective
- cures
- reverses
- prevents
- no side effects

require unusually strong, directly relevant evidence and careful context. Avoid promotional phrasing.

## Regulatory language

Regulatory status is time-sensitive and jurisdiction-specific. Store:
- jurisdiction
- status
- source
- checked date

## Corrections

Material errors should:
1. be corrected promptly
2. preserve revision history
3. identify what changed
4. trigger re-review of dependent publications

## Quotation policy

Prefer paraphrase with exact citations. Use short quotations only when wording itself is important.

## AI usage

AI can:
- organize
- propose summaries
- normalize metadata
- flag contradictions
- draft based on reviewed records

AI cannot:
- invent missing evidence
- autonomously approve publication
- silently infer a protocol
- replace scientific/clinical review
