# CARD 2 — YOUR BODY ALREADY USES PEPTIDES
## Your body already uses peptides.

Your body makes many different peptides of its own. Some act as hormones. Some help cells communicate in the nervous system. Others are part of the body’s natural defence against microbes.

### THE KEY IDEA
Peptides are already part of normal human biology — but that does not tell us what any peptide product will do.

**Visual direction:** Premium 3D human/cellular visualization showing three neutral areas: hormone signalling, brain/nerves, and body-surface defence. No syringes. No product imagery. No benefits language.

# CARD 3 — BUILDING BLOCKS
## Amino acids are the building blocks.

Amino acids are small molecules that can connect together in a specific order.

### AMINO ACID + AMINO ACID + AMINO ACID → PEPTIDE

Change the order, and you make a different peptide.

Peptides are generally shorter chains than proteins, but there is no single perfect number where a peptide suddenly becomes a protein.

**Visual direction:** Premium 3D amino-acid nodes assembling into a short peptide chain, then a longer folded protein form. Use a soft dashed transition instead of a hard numeric cutoff.

# CARD 4 — SIGNALS
## Many peptides act as signals. Not all do.

A signalling peptide can be released by one cell and recognised by another.

The peptide does not carry a complete set of instructions. Instead, it interacts with a receiving cell — and that cell creates the response.

### THE SIMPLE VERSION
Signal → receiver → response

**Visual direction:** Elegant 3D cell A releasing a small peptide-like signal toward cell B. Cell B has a visible receiving structure. A nearby cell without the right receptor remains unchanged.

---

# CARD 5 — RECEPTORS
## First it has to fit. Then something has to happen.

Cells have proteins called **receptors** that can recognise particular chemical signals.

Some molecules bind and switch a receptor on. Some switch it on only partly. Others can bind without switching it on — and may block another signal instead.

### FIT ≠ ACTIVATE

**Visual direction:** Three premium 3D receptor panels: FIT + ON, FIT + PARTLY ON, FIT + BLOCKS. Avoid childish literal keys. Use sophisticated molecular docking / switch imagery.

---

# CARD 6 — CONTEXT
## The same signal can mean different things to different cells.

A cell’s response depends on the receptors it has, its internal biology, and the other signals it is receiving at the same time.

The body also knows how to turn signals down or off. Receptors can become less responsive, and peptides can be broken down by enzymes.

### CONTEXT MATTERS.

**Visual direction:** One signal reaching two different cell types with distinct responses, plus a subtle fade/off sequence showing the signal being cleared.

# CARD 7 — WHERE IT HAPPENS
## Peptide signalling happens throughout the body.

The body uses peptide signalling in many places — including hormone systems, the brain and nerves, immune defence at body surfaces, pregnancy, blood-sugar control, calcium balance and blood-vessel regulation.

These are examples of normal biology — **not a list of benefits from peptide products.**

**Visual direction:** Premium neutral human-body map with elegant callouts. Labels only. No arrows suggesting treatment. No product vials, syringes, or benefit icons.

# CARD 8 — WHERE PEPTIDES COME FROM
## A peptide can come from the body, nature or a laboratory.

Scientists study peptides found in humans and other living things. They can also make peptides in laboratories for research and medicine.

A lab-made peptide might:

### MATCH
a natural sequence

### MODIFY
that sequence on purpose

### USE A FRAGMENT
of a longer natural peptide

“Lab-made” tells you where it came from — not whether it works or whether it is safe.

**Visual direction:** Three premium scientific pathways converging: body / other living sources / laboratory. Below, show same chain, modified chain, and fragment chain.

# CARD 9 — EVIDENCE
## “It’s a peptide” tells you almost nothing about the evidence.

Every peptide has its own research story.

Some have been studied in people. Others are known mainly from animal or cell research. Some are discussed mostly in clinical practice or reference material.

### STUDIED IN PEOPLE
### STUDIED IN ANIMALS OR CELLS
### DESCRIBED IN PRACTICE / REFERENCE WORKS

What is known about one peptide does not automatically apply to another peptide, another route, or another use.

**Visual direction:** Three elegant evidence lanes with several unnamed peptide tokens showing different amounts of evidence. No compound names on this page.

# CARD 10 — BRIDGE TO PDF 02
## A peptide molecule is only part of the story.

A common laboratory method builds a peptide chain step by step.

But the first result is not automatically a finished product. Synthesis can create a **crude mixture**, which then has to be purified and checked.

### BUILD → CRUDE MIXTURE → PURIFY → CHECK

Those checks answer different questions:

**IDENTITY** — What is it?  
**PURITY** — What else is present?  
**AMOUNT** — How much is there?
